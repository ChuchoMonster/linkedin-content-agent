# Op-ed themes

The author's opinion positions. **Status: live in the daily run.** Approved
op-ed drafts sit in `drafts/oped/queue/` and run first. How the run uses this
file is in `05-daily-run.md`, under "Op-ed days".

> This public copy uses generic example themes. The structure is the real one;
> the positions are placeholders to show how the file constrains the agent.

Cadence: 4 a week, Monday to Thursday in the 2:30 PM slot, replacing 4 of the 10
news posts.

**This file is the only source of the author's opinions.** The run argues these
positions and nothing else. New themes get added here by the author, never by
the agent.

A theme's points can each be their own post, or two or three can combine into one.

## 1. Safety talk can double as a competitive moat

Large incumbents can benefit when the public pushes for heavy regulation,
because compliance costs fall hardest on smaller competitors.

- A. Compliance overhead is a fixed cost. Fixed costs favor whoever is biggest.
- B. Calls for caution rarely come with a plan to slow the caller's own roadmap.
- C. Rules written today tend to describe today's market leaders, which makes
  them a wall for whoever comes next.

**Fact-check guidance:**
- Separate what an organization has said from what it has funded or lobbied
  for. They often point in different directions; check both before writing.
- Never assert as fact that a named person or company acted in bad faith. Say
  the incentive exists, and include any denial.

## 2. Automation hits physical work harder than it looks

- A. A lot of knowledge work has a person on the other end who is paid partly
  for the relationship. AI makes that person faster rather than redundant.
- B. Work that is bought purely for the output has no relationship to fall
  back on once a machine can produce the output.
- C. Headline job numbers can rise while specific roles shrink. That is
  displacement, not destruction.

**Fact-check guidance:** use the most recent official jobs report and note any
revisions to prior months. Don't call one strong month a trend.

## 3. Model makers will compete in every industry

- A. When the core product commoditizes, margin moves downstream.
- B. The cheapest way to find downstream margin is to build products in other
  people's industries.
- C. Every price cut on the core product makes that move more likely, not less.

**Fact-check guidance:** distinguish a company's own words from the press's
summary of them. Quote the source, not the headline.

## Tension to keep in view

The voice file's POV #1 is "bet on the best brain." Theme 3 says the core
product commoditizes. Both can be true (the best today, a commodity over time),
but a draft shouldn't claim both in one post without bridging them.
