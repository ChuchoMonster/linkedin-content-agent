# Before / after — the Gemini post

The machine's draft, then John's rewrite of it, 2026-08-15. This is the highest
value example in the system: it is the same idea written twice, once by the
machine and once by him. When a new draft feels off, diff it against this.

## What the machine wrote

> Gemini just hit a billion monthly users. It's not the best model.
>
> Google announced it August 11. Fastest-growing product they have ever shipped, and the 14th to reach a billion people. ChatGPT crossed the same line in June.
>
> Here's the part I keep chewing on. Depending on the day and the benchmark, Gemini is the third or fourth best AI platform out there. It still got to a billion faster than anything Google has ever built.
>
> Two years ago I'd have told you to niche down and pick a lane. Then I took on a project in an industry I knew nothing about going in.
>
> The demand isn't sitting at the frontier. It's everywhere, and most of it doesn't care which model is winning this week.
>
> Anybody else seeing this with clients, or is it just me?

## What John published

> Gemini just hit a billion monthly users and it's not even the best model!
>
> Gemini is the fastest-growing product Google has ever shipped, and the 14th to reach a billion people.
>
> Here's the crazy part-- depending on the day and the benchmark, Gemini is the third or fourth best AI platform out there. It still got to a billion faster than anything Google has ever built.
>
> A lot of the growth has to do with Google embedding Gemini into its core products, but still, this underscores how big AI already is and how much TAM is still out there.
>
> Ignore the AI doomers, this is the top of the 1st inning.

## The five changes, and the rule each one produces

**1. He joined the opener into one sentence and put an exclamation point on it.**
`Gemini just hit a billion monthly users. It's not the best model.` became
`Gemini just hit a billion monthly users and it's not even the best model!`
Two hard declaratives became one lighter line. Note `not even` doing the work
that the period was doing before.
→ Join clauses with `and` / `but` instead of chopping into separate declaratives.
Exclamation points are in bounds.

**2. He cut the announcement date.**
`Google announced it August 11` had nothing to do with the point. It was in the
draft only because it was a fact that existed.
→ A fact earns its place by serving the one idea. Having a fact is not a reason
to use it.

**3. He cut the ChatGPT comparison too.** Same reason. The post is about Gemini's
reach, not a race.

**4. He deleted the personal story entirely.** The niching / new-industry
paragraph was well-formed and still came out, because it did not fit this piece.
→ The story bank is an option, never a quota. A post with no story card in it is
a finished post.

**5. He conceded the objection, then ended on a verdict.**
He added `A lot of the growth has to do with Google embedding Gemini into its core
products, but still...` — naming the obvious counter-argument before pushing past
it. Then he closed on a call, not a question: `Ignore the AI doomers, this is the
top of the 1st inning.`
→ Answer the smart reader's objection inside the post. Closing on a question is
one option, not the default.

## Register note

`Here's the part I keep chewing on` became `Here's the crazy part--`. Shorter,
more casual, less writerly. The machine reaches for the considered phrase; he
reaches for the fast one.
